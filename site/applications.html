BEGINNER IDEA

A WiFi signal is also a weak sensor

WiFi devices send radio waves. People, furniture, walls, and motion change how those waves travel before they reach another device.

Channel State Information, in plain language

CSI is a detailed measurement of how a WiFi channel changes a known signal as it travels from transmitter to receiver. It is usually recorded across many subcarriers and antenna links.

For sensing, we look for patterns in CSI amplitude and phase over time. Breathing, walking, falls, gestures, or room occupancy can leave different patterns in the wireless channel.

CSI

Fine-grained channel measurements, richer than one signal-strength number.

Subcarrier

One small frequency slice inside a WiFi channel. A recording can track many slices at once.

Amplitude

How strong the channel response is at a moment, subcarrier, and antenna link.

Phase

The signal shift. Useful, but often needs careful calibration or cleaning.

APPLICATION MAP

From lab benchmarks to downstream scenarios

WiSenseHub can serve domain experts who know the sensing problem, even if they are not wireless researchers.

Clinical and care settings

Vitals, falls, mobility

Explore breathing, fall detection, gait, and activity patterns as non-contact signals. Treat outputs as research data, not clinical decisions.

Smart home

Presence and routines

Study occupancy, motion, daily activities, and context changes using existing wireless infrastructure.

Human-computer interaction

Gestures and behavior

Analyze gestures, body motion, and device-free interaction without cameras or wearables.

Security and safety

Intrusion and anomaly cues

Investigate coarse motion, through-obstacle changes, and environment-aware monitoring.

Education

Signal + data literacy

Let students collect simple wireless traces, see tensors, plot signals, and compare preprocessing choices.

Benchmarking

Reusable research data

Use shared schemas and splits so results are easier to compare across datasets and tasks.

Important boundary

WiFi sensing is promising, but deployment remains sensitive to hardware, room layout, people, privacy expectations, and data quality. WiSenseHub helps organize and inspect data; it does not certify a medical, safety, or consumer product.
